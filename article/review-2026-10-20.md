# Revision brief: cohort behavior, aggregation and profitability decisions

Status: needs revision. This brief specifies changes; it does not claim that the simulator or charts have been rebuilt.

Reviewed on 2026-10-20 against commit [5bcff2c](https://github.com/rothnic/bayesian-email-cohort-lab/tree/5bcff2cd4a4b6a7edda5556ea2d64f274b5bb7b0). The published article, simulation, model and saved forecasts at that commit were inspected. No new model fits or experiments were run for this review.

## What the current implementation can and cannot show

The data are stochastic. Source and cohort response amplitudes vary, daily human and bot events follow negative-binomial draws, payment amounts vary, and response/CPC have shared weekly disturbances. Scenario-specific prices and human performance also change over calendar time.

However, every cohort has the same prescribed age shape:

    f(age) = 0.75 exp(-age / 3) + 0.25 exp(-age / 90).

The response-amplitude lognormal standard deviation is 0.20, corresponding to approximately 20.2% between-cohort coefficient of variation in that amplitude. This changes a cohort's scale, not its intrinsic decay speed. The fixed-price control still contains random calendar disturbances and source/cohort variation; it removes systematic economic deterioration rather than fixing every realized value.

The dynamic simulation records aggregate cohort-day response events. Repeats are allowed, accepted exposure remains the original lead count, and there are no stable individual IDs, eligibility histories, person-level churn or reactivation. Event totals divided by acquired leads are not unique-person retention. Weekly and monthly distinct people cannot be recovered from the existing event totals.

## Define the metric before aggregating

Use two separately named measures:

1. **Engagement retention:** distinct qualified human leads with at least one response in a specified age window, divided by all leads originally acquired in that cohort. The original denominator includes inactive and nonhuman acquisitions because they incurred acquisition cost.
2. **Contactability:** the fraction still subscribed and eligible to receive mail. This is distinct from activity: a contactable person can be inactive and later return.

Start with seven-day age windows. For cohort c, age window k, and information cutoff T:

    R(c,k;T) = U(c,k;T) / N(c)

N(c) is original acquisitions. U is the distinct qualified-human count in that window using information available by T. In a controlled first example, qualification may be assumed known. With incomplete or noisy audits, show observed qualification coverage and infer the uncertain human count; never present hidden simulator human labels as observed evidence. Event-level audit fractions cannot simply be multiplied by a distinct-person count to solve this.

Define acquisition-vintage grouping separately from the activity window. Grouping cohorts by acquisition week or calendar month can retain the same seven-day engagement measure. Switching to monthly active people changes the measure and requires distinct IDs across the entire month, not the sum of weekly unique counts.

For an acquisition group G at the same age window:

    R(G,k;T) = sum U(c,k;T) / sum N(c), over eligible cohorts c in G.

Pool numerators and denominators; do not average percentages unless that weighting is intentional. For example, 20 active people from 100 acquisitions and 90 from 900 give 110/1000 = 11%, not 15%. This is illustrative arithmetic, not a new simulation result.

Only complete, sufficiently reported age windows enter an observed point. Younger cohorts are missing at later ages, never zero. Show the contributing cohort count and original-lead denominator at every age. An all-history curve has a changing vintage mix at older ages; pair it with recent-vintage curves and use a fixed mature cohort set when comparing curve shapes.

Require exclusive cohort membership, or define reacquisition episodes and revenue attribution explicitly. Deduplicate within the full activity window before rolling up. Cumulative ever-engaged share is another measure and must not be shown as declining retention.

## Change the generator and inference together

Minimum changes for a genuine retention demonstration:

- Stable synthetic person, acquisition and event IDs; acquisition/source membership; occurrence, reporting, qualification and settlement clocks; and eligibility history. Publish aggregate summaries, keeping the six previously excluded row-level exports excluded.
- Persistent person-level heterogeneity and cohort/source differences in decay behavior, rather than only independent count noise around one universal curve.
- Unequal cohort sizes so the weighting example is exercised. Include zero-activity windows and genuinely immature cohorts.
- A clear distinction between permanent unsubscribe/bounce, temporary inactivity and reactivation if reactivation is included.
- Calendar changes and shocks in performance and revenue, retained acquisition quotes, and optional correlated response/value shocks. Historical paid costs remain fixed.
- Inference that estimates relevant retention/age parameters and uncertainty rather than being given the generator's exact age curve. State constraints on age, period and acquisition-vintage effects: calendar date equals acquisition date plus cohort age, so unrestricted effects cannot all be separately identified.

Begin with a controlled measurable case, then introduce changing prices, declining quality, imperfect audits and a new source as matched extensions. Do not add all latent mechanisms at once and assume a more complex model is better calibrated.

Verify that replicated data reproduce the observed distribution of cohort curves, zero periods, decay differences and aggregate behavior. These are posterior predictive checks, not a replacement for forecasting evaluation. See [Stan's predictive-check guidance](https://mc-stan.org/docs/stan-users-guide/posterior-predictive-checks.html).

## Replace the explanation sequence

| View | Required content | Reader's question |
|---|---|---|
| Individual cohort spaghetti | Thin observed curves by cohort; cohort age on X; seven-day engagement retention on Y; source facets and acquisition-vintage colors. End each observed curve at its information boundary. | How differently do acquired cohorts behave? |
| The same data aggregated | Acquisition-week, acquisition-month and all-history summaries using pooled counts and denominators, with cohort/lead counts shown. Preserve recent-vintage comparison. | What does averaging clarify, and what deterioration can it hide? |
| One cohort's possible economic futures | Observed settled contribution, pending-report region, observation cutoff, faint sampled predictive paths, median and interval; money per original acquired lead on Y. | How does activity turn into profit or loss? |
| The same cohort learning over time | Fixed target outcome; probability versus successive information cutoffs/cohort ages; global-only, source-sharing and cohort-specific fits evaluated with the same available evidence and economics. | When does the evidence become sufficient? |
| Reliability of the declaration | Held-out predicted-probability bins versus realized outcomes; first-declaration errors, decision ages and independent-world uncertainty. | Should a claim of high confidence be believed? |

Observed cohort spaghetti consists of different cohorts. Predictive spaghetti consists of possible futures for one selected cohort. Label those distinctly. An interval for a historical/population average is not the predictive spread of a new cohort.

Total activity by calendar date may remain a small contextual inset. New arrivals can raise total activity while engagement per acquired lead deteriorates. Put hierarchy equations and latent log-state plots after the reader understands these behavioral and decision views.

## Define a profitability decision, not just a payback curve

Use a declared horizon H, minimum contribution buffer delta, sending policy and future-price scenario:

    p(c,T) = P[M(c,H)/N(c) > delta | information available by T, policy, scenario].

A first demonstration can retain H = 180 to match existing output. Export and separately evaluate terminal age-425 profitability if that is also needed. Delta = 0 means positive contribution; a positive delta means meeting a higher target.

An illustrative review rule is:

- At least 95%: candidate declaration that the cohort will meet the target.
- At most 5%: candidate declaration that it will not meet the target.
- Between them: insufficient evidence.

These are proposed thresholds, not calibrated guarantees or universally optimal business rules. State the cost of false positive and false negative decisions and inspect expected downside as well. Bayesian action selection can use expected utility/loss, as described in [Stan's decision-analysis guidance](https://mc-stan.org/docs/stan-users-guide/decision-analysis.html).

Predeclare weekly review dates. The first qualifying review is a candidate decision age; it is not retrospectively the exact earliest crossing between reviews. Some cohorts will remain undecidable. Allow later evidence or a changed price regime to reverse the assessment.

Separate reviews before cohort age H (prospective forecasts) from reviews at or after H while reports remain incomplete (nowcasts of an already occurred outcome). Report their decision ages separately; do not count a late settlement classification as advance forecasting success.

Keep these events separate:

- Positive expected contribution.
- Positive realized contribution at a fixed horizon.
- First crossing of zero by that horizon.
- Remaining nonnegative after a crossing.

The existing p_positive180 measures the second event at age 180. The existing p_payback425 measures at least one crossing, not positive terminal contribution at 425. The current buy rule uses positive sampled mean day-180 margin, not a 95% probability threshold. Current future-scenario probabilities are conditional; do not turn one assumed future into unconditional confidence across unknown price regimes.

A fresh purchase includes its quoted acquisition cost. A continue-sending decision for an acquired cohort compares incremental future revenue and avoidable costs; acquisition is already sunk. Label retrospective cohort profitability and these action decisions separately.

## The existing probabilities need validation

An additional read-only diagnostic of the accepted held-out file found:

- 306 dynamic-model forecast rows across 18 independent worlds.
- 123 rows with p_positive180 at least 0.95.
- 17 of those rows have nonpositive realized day-180 contribution.

These rows repeat cohorts and cutoffs and are correlated. The counts are descriptive evidence, not an independently estimated first-declaration error rate. They are also conditional forecasts scored against the declared stress worlds, including future scenarios that differ from a hold-current assumption.

The original equal-world nominal 95% interval coverage of 56.5% remains relevant. Interval coverage and probability calibration are different checks; neither should be substituted for the other.

Diagnostic source: [heldout_scores.csv](../extensions/calendar/dynamic_lab/artifacts/heldout_scores.csv), SHA256 465f9d3b2af357075843b53d38fc65f153f5494bc8610936f5a18c682dd574e7.

Validate the proposed rule by replaying every prespecified weekly review using only records visible at that time. Freeze the rule and use new independent held-out seeds after model development. Score one first declaration per cohort and policy, then report false profitable declarations, false unprofitable declarations, undecided cohorts, decision delay and economic loss. Retain reversals. Cluster uncertainty by independent world, preserving dependence among cohorts and reviews.

With 256 predictive draws, probability estimates near 95% also have Monte Carlo uncertainty. Increase the draw budget or mark borderline classifications unresolved; do not select a dramatic threshold crossing from simulation noise.

## Acceptance checks for the next implementation

- Known small fixtures prove distinct-person counting, weekly/monthly deduplication, weighted rollups, changing age support and missing-versus-zero behavior.
- Generated cohorts differ in their latent retention shapes and realized histories; stochasticity remains when deterministic deterioration is disabled.
- Observed panels exclude future reports and evaluator-only truth. Known-human and noisy-audit cases are distinguished.
- All aggregation modes reconcile to the same underlying records and measure definitions.
- A selected cohort is held fixed throughout the learning plot. Its target horizon, cost basis and future assumptions are visible.
- Profit probabilities, payback probabilities and mean-profit decisions have separate exports and checks.
- First-declaration reliability is scored across untouched worlds and price/quality stresses, rather than inferred from a few favorable examples.
- Joint portfolio forecasts preserve shared calendar risk. More cohorts may reduce independent noise without eliminating common price or performance shocks.
- The article, Markdown/MDX, HTML, figures, tables, source hashes and captions are regenerated together. Until that is done, this brief is a specification rather than an implemented revision.
